# Editorial Journeys

Between the Climbs — Creative Director Master Prompt for Lovable

Project Overview

Build a premium editorial website for Between the Climbs, a publication by Joy D'Souza.

This is not a portfolio, personal brand site, corporate website, or blog template.

It is a digital publication designed to become a destination that readers intentionally return to because of the quality of its stories, ideas, and reading experience.

The design should feel timeless today and still feel relevant ten years from now.

The guiding principle behind every decision is:

Capture the eye within five seconds. Capture the mind within thirty seconds. Capture the heart before visitors leave.

The experience should feel like opening a beautifully designed literary journal rather than browsing a website.

The Brand

Between the Climbs

by Joy D'Souza

Writer, entrepreneur, investment professional, and recovering perfectionist.

Exploring reinvention, work, money, motherhood, mountains, and the stories we tell ourselves while figuring life out.

Core Brand Idea

The publication is built around one central metaphor:

Life is not defined by its summits.

It is shaped by everything that happens between the climbs.

Every page should reinforce this idea through storytelling, imagery, spacing, motion, typography, and navigation.

This website should feel like a quiet place where ambitious, thoughtful people come to pause, reflect, and continue their journey.

Primary Goal

Design a publication that visitors return to even when they are not specifically looking for Joy D'Souza.

The stories are the product.

Joy is the editor and guide.

Audience

Build for thoughtful readers who enjoy long-form writing and reflective ideas.

They are professionals, entrepreneurs, parents, outdoor enthusiasts, lifelong learners, and people navigating seasons of change.

They appreciate depth over speed and quality over quantity.

Website Architecture

Create the following pages:

Home

Stories

About Joy

Speaking

Projects

Newsletter

LilMissBelle Archive (external link)

Contact

The navigation should remain intentionally minimal.

Stories should always be the primary destination.

Speaking, projects, and future initiatives should feel like natural extensions of the publication rather than competing priorities.

Homepage Experience

The homepage should unfold like a carefully edited magazine.

Create the following sections in this order:

1. Full-Screen Editorial Hero

A cinematic mountain landscape at sunrise.

Large editorial typography.

Headline introducing the philosophy of Between the Climbs.

Supporting copy.

Two calls to action:

Read the Stories

Join the Journey

2. A Note from Joy

A short editorial welcome explaining why this publication exists.

This should feel personal, sincere, and inviting.

3. Featured Story

One large editorial feature with immersive imagery.

Display reading time, category, and a compelling excerpt.

4. Latest Stories

Magazine-style editorial grid.

Avoid generic blog cards.

Focus on typography and imagery.

5. Reflection Quote

Create a full-width pull-quote section with generous whitespace.

Typography should become the visual focus.

6. Speaking

Present speaking as an extension of Joy's ideas rather than a commercial service.

Include a clear "Book Joy to Speak" CTA.

7. Current & Upcoming Projects

Display:

Weekly Newsletter

Entrepreneurship Series

Children's Financial Literacy

Rapporteur Work

Book Launch

Use elegant editorial cards.

Each should include meaningful placeholder copy and a subtle "Coming Soon" treatment.

8. Newsletter

This should be one of the most beautiful sections on the site.

The newsletter should feel like joining an ongoing conversation, not subscribing to marketing emails.

Headline suggestion:

Walk the Next Stretch Together

9. LilMissBelle Archive

Introduce Joy's earlier writing.

Present it as the foundation that eventually became Between the Climbs.

Provide a button linking to the external blog.

10. Footer

Minimal.

Editorial.

Calm.

End with:

Until the next climb.

UX Principles

The website should never feel overwhelming.

Every page should answer one question:

What should the visitor explore next?

Every article should recommend:

Three related stories

The newsletter

Another relevant section

There should be no dead ends anywhere on the site.

Motion Design

Motion should feel calm and almost invisible.

Use:

Gentle fade-ins

Smooth scrolling

Elegant hover states

Slow image reveals

Reading progress indicator

Soft navigation transitions

Avoid dramatic movement.

Design Quality Standard

Before finalizing the design, review every section against these questions:

Does this feel like a publication rather than a personal website?

Is there enough whitespace to create a calm reading experience?

Would this layout still look elegant in ten years?

Does every section support the central metaphor of "Between the Climbs"?

Is the typography doing enough of the storytelling?

Does the imagery feel authentic?

Is there a clear next step without being pushy?

Would a visitor want to linger here?

If any answer is "no," refine the design before considering it complete.

Final Creative Direction

Don't optimize for novelty.

Optimize for timelessness.

Build a website that feels like the digital equivalent of finding a beautifully written journal in a quiet mountain lodge—a place people return to because it consistently offers perspective, calm, and meaningful stories.

The final experience should leave visitors thinking:

"I came here to read one story. I stayed because this place made me slow down. I'll come back because I know there will always be another thoughtful story waiting for me."

One addition I'd make

I'd also instruct Lovable to avoid placeholder-looking "Coming Soon" sections. Instead, each future initiative should read like an editor's note about what's being developed and why it matters. That way, even unfinished sections contribute to the publication's narrative instead of feeling empty. It's a small detail, but it reinforces that every part of the site has intention.
